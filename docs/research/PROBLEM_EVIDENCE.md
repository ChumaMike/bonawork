# Problem evidence

Status: research note for the pitch and for engineering. All sources were opened and read on 16 November 2026 unless a row says otherwise.

**How to read this file.** Every claim is in one of four labelled sections: documented facts (section 2), product hypotheses (section 3), proposed benefits (section 4) and unverified claims (section 5). Only section 2 may be stated as fact on a slide or in front of judges.

## 1. The problem in one paragraph

South Africa already collects reports about broken school infrastructure. What is missing is a trustworthy answer to "was it actually fixed?". In March 2026 Parliament's own education committee said it could not accept the department's infrastructure data as a true reflection of what members see in schools, and warned that incomplete classrooms may be recorded as completed [S1]. A 2026 University of Johannesburg study of 7,282 South Africans concluded that the country has "a response problem", not a reporting problem [S8]. BonaBlock for Schools targets that one gap: the moment a repair is declared done by the same party that was responsible for doing it.

## 3. Product hypotheses

These are our beliefs. None is proven. Each has a test in `docs/research/VALIDATION.md`.

| #   | Hypothesis                                                                                                                                         | Why we believe it         | What would disprove it                                                       |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| H1  | The weakest point in the repair lifecycle is closure: whoever is responsible for the fix is also the one who records it as done.                   | F3, F10, F15              | Stakeholders tell us closure is already checked by a separate party.         |
| H2  | A second, separately accountable person confirming or disputing completion would change behaviour, even with no new enforcement power.             | Reasoning from F6 and F13 | Reviewers say a dispute has no consequence, so nobody acts on it.            |
| H3  | Parents and school governing body members will report and endorse if they can see what happened to the report.                                     | F21, F22                  | Low reporting in a pilot despite visible status.                             |
| H4  | District officials will accept a reviewer role if it takes under ten minutes per case on a phone.                                                  | No evidence yet           | Officials refuse, or say they have no mandate.                               |
| H5  | A public, tamper-evident record is more persuasive to oversight bodies than a departmental database export.                                        | F2, F4                    | Oversight staff say they would not consult it.                               |
| H6  | School administrators will take part even though disputes are visible, because a verified fix protects them from blame for work they did complete. | No evidence yet           | Administrators see only risk and decline.                                    |
| H7  | Automated triage (category, severity, duplicate detection) saves enough administrator time to matter.                                              | No evidence yet           | Administrators say report volume per school is too low for triage to matter. |

## 4. Proposed benefits

These follow only if the hypotheses hold. Phrase them in the pitch as "we expect" or "designed to", never "BonaBlock reduces" or "BonaBlock improves".

- **For learners and parents:** a report cannot quietly disappear, and "fixed" means a second person checked.
- **For school administrators:** proof of work actually done, and a dated record of when an issue was escalated beyond the school.
- **For district reviewers:** one queue of completion claims with evidence attached, instead of ad hoc site reports.
- **For oversight bodies** (Parliament, the Auditor-General, civil society): a record whose integrity they can check themselves, without trusting BonaBlock or the department.

We have no measured outcome for any of these. There has been no pilot.
